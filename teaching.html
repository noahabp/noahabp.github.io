Teaching

I study how organizations navigate strategic transitions, and the academy is in the middle of exactly such a transition: generative AI is reshaping post-secondary education. My research is not separate from my teaching—it informs how I adapt to this technology and harness it, rather than work around it.

I served as a course assistant (2025) and teaching assistant (2026) for Stanford’s MS&E 180 (Organizations: Theory and Management), delivering instruction over multiple course meetings and earning excellent course evaluations.

MS&E 180 · Organizations: Theory and Management

MS&E 180 introduces fundamental theories from organizational, behavioral, and social science research and shows how they are used to understand and improve organizations. The course moves from individuals to groups to organizations—emphasizing high-technology and other commercial firms, though the material applies to all kinds of organizations, including non-profits and government—and concludes with a module on technology (e.g., AI) and its impact on the future of work and organizations.

MS&E 288 · Managing Innovation and Driving Adoption of Frontier Technologies

Guest instructor · 2026

I co-designed and helped run a board-of-directors simulation. Students used AI to help draft a one-page memo evaluating an investment decision on a frontier technology, kept a log of the prompts that mattered and what they trusted, challenged, or verified, and then wrote a short reflection on where the tool helped and where it created risk. A week later, they sat as boards of six with an instructor as the chair, each member holding their own memo and recommendation, and they had to reach a decision together. While their memos could be generated, understanding could not be faked in a fast-moving, high-level discussion.

I have also co-organized the lab meeting of the Center for Work, Technology, and Organization (WTO), helping socialize new doctoral students into the program, teaching them to position and frame theoretical contributions, and preparing them for their comprehensive exams.

Earlier, at Harvard Business School, I served as a course assistant for Finance 1 and, as a research associate to Joshua Coval and Malcolm Baker, helped redesign the course’s case curriculum.
